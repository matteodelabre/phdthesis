\chapter*{Abstract}

Genes are the carriers of heredity in living organisms.
Over time and through various events, they can evolve into different alleles and spread across multiple species.
Phylogenetics aims to trace back the evolutionary history of units such as genes, cells, or species.
The evolution of a gene depends not only on the events directly affecting it, but also on the evolution of its host species.
To study these evolutionary relationships, the so-called \emph{reconciliation} method was introduced.
When a host carries multiple families of dependent units, their evolutionary trajectories become intertwined.
Several approaches exist to extend reconciliation to this type of situation.

This thesis shows that such a multiple reconciliation approach, called segmental reconciliation, can model complex evolutionary events such as segmental duplications or horizontal transfers.
However, when developing this model, we encounter a combinatorial explosion in the number of solutions constituting the space of possible reconciliations.
To ensure the interpretability of results obtained through this model, one of the objectives of this thesis is also to conduct a systematic analysis of this solution space.
To do so, we adopt an algebraic approach that demonstrates that this kind of question can be answered generically for a wide variety of problems.
